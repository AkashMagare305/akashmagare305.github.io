01

Automotive Paint Shop Engineering

Surface preparation • Pretreatment • E-coat • Painting • Inspection
+

Study the complete automotive paint shop process, from incoming body inspection and surface preparation to final paint quality evaluation.

01. Paint Shop Fundamentals

Purpose of automotive painting, corrosion protection, appearance requirements, process flow, paint materials and basic terminology.

02. Pretreatment Process

Cleaning, degreasing, rinsing, surface conditioning, conversion coating, bath monitoring concepts and process purpose.

03. Electro-Deposition (ED)

ED coating principles, bath composition concepts, film formation, ultrafiltration, rinsing stages, curing and inspection.

04. Paint Mixing Room

Paint storage, material identification, batch traceability, viscosity concepts, mixing records, filtration and material handling.

05. Primer & Top Coat

Primer purpose, basecoat, clearcoat, application methods, film appearance, colour matching and coating characteristics.

06. Paint Quality Inspection

Visual inspection, gloss, colour variation, film thickness, adhesion testing concepts and defect classification.

07. Paint Shop Equipment

Conveyors, spray booths, ovens, air handling units, filtration systems, pumps, robot systems and process utilities.

08. Paint Shop Documentation

SOP, process flow diagrams, control plans, inspection standards, check sheets and maintenance records.

Learning objective: Understand the purpose and quality requirements of each paint shop stage. Actual industrial operations must follow approved plant procedures, training and safety controls.

Paint Quality Reference

Automotive Paint Defect Library.

An introduction to common paint appearance and coating defects. Detailed inspection criteria depend on the applicable product specification.

Orange Peel

Uneven surface texture that resembles the surface of an orange.

Dust & Dirt

Foreign particles visible within or on the coating surface.

Runs & Sags

Excess coating accumulation that creates an uneven flow pattern.

Pinholes

Small visible holes or openings in the coating film.

Blistering

Raised areas or bubbles that affect coating appearance.

Colour Variation

Visible difference in colour compared with the approved standard.

Fisheyes

Small circular depressions in the coating surface.

Paint Sagging

Local downward movement of wet coating before it cures.

Low Gloss

Appearance with lower-than-required surface reflectivity.

Scratch Marks

Visible surface damage affecting the coating appearance.

Cratering

Small bowl-shaped depressions in the coating film.

Adhesion Failure

Loss of bonding between coating layers or between coating and substrate.

Quality Management Toolkit

Seven Basic Quality Control Tools.

Learn how basic quality tools help teams organize data, identify patterns, investigate problems and monitor processes.

01. Check Sheet

Structured data collection for defects, events and observations.

02. Pareto Chart

Prioritization of important categories using frequency or impact.

03. Fishbone Diagram

Organizing possible causes under categories such as Man, Machine, Method and Material.

04. Histogram

Visualizing the distribution and spread of measured data.

05. Control Chart

Monitoring process behaviour over time.

06. Scatter Diagram

Exploring relationships between two variables.

07. Stratification

Separating data into meaningful groups to identify patterns.

Additional: 8D

A structured team-based approach to solving quality problems.
